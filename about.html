A-THRICE / ARTIST NOTE

About the project

Family photographs are the starting point, not anonymous case studies.

Artist Statement

I began with photographs of my own family taken at weddings: the ceremonies, the gatherings around them and the moments of being together. I wanted to look at the strange contrast between the richness of these relationships and the narrow categories used to describe complexions in Indian matrimonial culture.

I gave each person a place in the seven-colour key. Some skin becomes a single flat field; some is left blank, marked only with the number of the shade that would fill it. The embroidered fabrics, jewellery, hair and expressions remain recognisable. Those details celebrate the people in the photographs. The chart does something else: it asks us to see them as categories.

A paint-by-numbers system seems harmless until the labels begin to resemble a system of social judgment. The categories are not scientific; the consistency is part of the fiction. I am interested in how easily an imposed measurement can be mistaken for an objective description.

The larger digital exhibition follows that judgment beyond the family album and into advertisements, corporate brands, beauty markets and health warnings. Companies have sold the possibility of becoming more desirable or successful through a change in complexion. I want to ask who benefits from that promise, and what we lose when belonging begins to resemble something that can be purchased.

The photographs are of real people I care about. This work is not a judgment of them. It is an attempt to make the system visible.
