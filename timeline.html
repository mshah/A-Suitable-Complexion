02 / THE HISTORY

From snow to glow

Long before the modern fairness cream, skin colour circulated through ideas of status and beauty. Industry made those ideas packageable, scalable and saleable.

Before industrial cosmeticsIndia / Asia
Precolonial / early modern

Beauty traditions precede commercial creams

Preferences for lighter skin appear in multiple historical contexts; the exhibition rejects a single-origin account.

Do not assign a universal start date to colourism. S02 S03

1890sUK / global
Late colonial period

Hazeline line sold as skin care

British manufacturer Burroughs Wellcome marketed Hazeline products first around hygiene and skin care, ahead of later beauty and whitening language.

Heritage/medical claims not identical to subsequent Indian ads. S03 S05

c. 1909UK / global
Late colonial period

Snow enters the copy

A British Hazeline Snow leaflet advertised the effect of making skin soft and white.

British item, not confirmed as India-specific. S05

1912India
Colonial India

Burroughs Wellcome opens Bombay branch

Global pharmaceutical advertising and local Indian marketing become connected through a formal branch.

Branch year documented in archival academic article. S03

1919India
Colonial India

Afghan Snow launches

E. S. Patanwala’s brand made a locally produced Indian beauty cream part of the category; snow imagery helped circulate complexion ideals.

1919 date is reported history; keep source attribution. S04 S31

1930India
Colonial India

Hazeline Snow in Indian press

The Hindu carried Hazeline Snow product advertising in October 1930.

Indian original reproduced in Cambridge article; reproduction clearance pending. S03

1937Japan
Parallel Asian history

Shiseido Whitening Cream

Shiseido lists a Whitening Cream within its modern facial-care programme.

Comparative case, not evidence of identical cultural dynamics. S06

1940sIndia
Colonial India

The modern woman appears in Hazeline advertising

Indian print advertisements increasingly paired creams with women and modern beauty imagery.

Historical interpretation drawn from academic archive research. S03

1947–1950India
Early postcolonial

Independence; whitening rhetoric survives

After independence, corporate discussions still promoted local appeal of Hazeline’s skin-whitening effect.

1950 company correspondence documented in archival research. S03

1975–1978India
Postcolonial mass market

Fair & Lovely reaches the market

Reporting dates pilot/test marketing to March 1975; HUL’s own anniversary chronology dates its launch to 1978.

Show both dates with source labels, not one artificially certain date. S07 S32

1990s–2000sIndia
Postcolonial mass market

From personal care to social aspiration

Beauty marketing links complexion with marriage, romance, self-confidence and work; competition expands.

Advertising messages are not proof of real economic returns. S08 S32

2008Philippines
Regional campaigns

GlutaMAX “From ebony to Ivory”

A documented ad uses a stark before/after complexion transformation.

Archival reproduction needs clearance. S25

2014India
Regulation and backlash

Fairness-advertising guidelines

ASCI warns against associating dark skin with unhappiness, inferior status, marriage disadvantage or reduced employment prospects.

A self-regulatory guideline, not proof violations ended. S08

2016Thailand
Regional campaigns

“White makes you win”

Seoul Secret’s Snowz supplement campaign causes criticism and is removed.

Avoid presenting one campaign as representative of all Thailand. S21

2019–2020India
Rebranding

Fair & Lovely becomes Glow & Lovely

HUL describes a 2019 messaging shift to glow and radiance, followed by 2020 renaming.

Company states formulation evolved and product is not bleaching cream. S09 S28

2023Global
Public health

Mercury rule strengthens

Minamata Convention parties decide to remove a 1ppm threshold from the cosmetics phase-out provision.

Implementation varies by country. S18

2024–2026UK
Consumer safety

Illegal-product warnings continue

Trading Standards warns about hazardous products; later alerts show illegal skin-lightening cosmetics in diverse retail settings and online.

Geographic UK case study, not Asia prevalence. S20 S29

2026 estimateGlobal / India
Current market

Two different market measures

Global skin-lightening forecast: US$13.2bn; entire India skincare estimate: US$2.98bn. These are not comparable categories.

Both figures from commercial firms; 2026 global forecast not observed revenue. S13 S14

No entries in this selection.

The history is not a single origin story.

Precolonial preferences, colonial power, local matrimonial cultures, modern corporate advertising and transnational beauty markets intersect. The timeline highlights a documented commercial history, without claiming that India or Asia is culturally uniform.
