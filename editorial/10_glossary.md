# The Language of Complexion

**Colourism.** Discrimination, prejudice or unequal treatment tied to gradations of skin colour, often within the same or related racial and ethnic groups. It intersects with class, caste, gender and other forms of hierarchy. [S02] [S11]

**Fair / fairness.** A term historically used in South Asian matrimonial and beauty-product advertising to indicate lighter complexion. In this context it carries social meanings beyond pigment alone. [S08] [S12]

**Wheatish.** A widely used and imprecise complexion descriptor. The series reproduces the documented branches Wheatish, Wheatish Medium, Wheatish Brown and Wheatish Dark as examples of a supposedly systematic taxonomy. [S23]

**Skin lightening / whitening / bleaching.** Broad commercial and colloquial terms for attempts to change skin pigmentation. Products vary substantially, so their active ingredients, regulation and risks must be described specifically. [S15] [S16]

**Brightening / glow / radiance.** Marketing words that can describe an even skin appearance, increased luminosity or treatment of dark spots, and can also carry the afterlife of earlier fairness ideals. They are not synonyms for one identical chemical formula. [S09]

**Hyperpigmentation.** Darkening of particular areas of skin resulting from several possible clinical or environmental causes. Treating a localized condition is distinct from stigmatizing someone's natural complexion. [S16]

**Industry estimate.** A market research firm's modeled measure for a defined set of products, distinct from audited corporate sales or official national accounts. [S13] [S14]

**Inelastic demand.** An economic concept meaning purchases respond comparatively little to changes in price. This exhibit does not assert that demand for skin-lightening creams is demonstrably inelastic; the relevant elasticity evidence has not been established. [S13]
