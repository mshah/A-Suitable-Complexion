# A Suitable Complexion

**Family photographs. Seven shades. A system of judgment.**

At weddings, on beaches, in living rooms, people gather to be remembered. The photographs preserve affection, jokes, awkward poses, clothing chosen for an occasion, and the simple fact of being together. In *A Suitable Complexion*, those photographs are interrupted by a colour chart.

Every person receives one of seven complexion categories. Some areas of skin are filled in a single flat colour; other people's skin is left unpainted, marked only by a number. Their expressions, clothing and relationships remain.

The categories come from the language of Indian matrimonial matching; the colours, numbering and method are the artist's invention. The result is not a measurement of a person. It is a picture of what measurement can do to a person.

## Beyond the family album

For generations, manufacturers have sold creams, soaps and serums by promising a change in complexion—and sometimes implying a change in prospects. The promise has appeared in the language of love, professional success, scientific progress and, more recently, glow. Behind an intimate habit sits an international industry. This website follows the relationship between the classification, the advertisement, the purchase and the person. [S02] [S03] [S09]

## Read before entering

The portraits depict real relatives. The numbered tones are deliberately arbitrary artistic labels, not medical, ethnic or biological classifications. Some original photographs include children. Publication and naming of each photograph are subject to family permissions; this draft website does not treat the source archive as publicly cleared.
