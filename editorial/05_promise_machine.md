# The Promise Machine
## What a Cream Was Asked to Deliver

An advertisement can attach an extraordinary future to an ordinary tube. A cream becomes a doorway to romance, a chance at recognition, a modern self, an office career. The message rarely needs to say aloud that darker skin is inferior. It can tell the story of an unhappy 'before' and a rewarded 'after' and let the viewer complete the sentence. [S08]

The historic record shows several languages layered over one another. Early commercial skin products circulated through medicine and hygiene; later Hazeline advertisements associated complexion with cultivated beauty. A Wellcome Collection leaflet dated approximately 1909 described Hazeline Snow as making the skin 'soft and white'. Decades later, the product name still carried the imagery of snow into global markets. [S02] [S05]

In the Philippines, a 2008 GlutaMAX advertisement represented an especially explicit transformation, using the phrase 'From ebony to Ivory' alongside before-and-after imagery. In Thailand, a 2016 Seoul Secret Snowz advertisement asserted 'White makes you win'. The latter was withdrawn after intense public criticism. These are documented historical slogans presented as evidence, not statements endorsed by the exhibition. [S21] [S25]

India's story includes a crucial change in corporate vocabulary. Fair & Lovely, sold from the 1970s, was renamed Glow & Lovely in 2020. The company's own history describes a turn toward 'glow', 'clarity' and 'radiance'. The shift invites a question rather than a predetermined answer: when a brand stops saying 'fair', how much of the earlier social promise has been abandoned, and how much has been translated? [S07] [S09]

### The archive should speak in its own voice

Every advertisement on this page should be labeled with the original country, approximate date, product, language, exact transcribed copy, archive source, rights status and a short critical caption. The historical advertisement should be visibly distinguished from the curatorial commentary. Do not retrofit modern words into old advertisements or quote a slogan without tracing it to a contemporaneous recording or reproducible source.

### Promises on the wall

*White makes you win* is not a recommendation: it is a primary example of what an advertiser once considered a commercially useful claim. Beside it should be less direct lines about charm, complexion, radiance and success. Together they reveal the distance between what people are entitled to and what marketing persuades them to purchase.

**Pull quote:** An advertisement does not have to guarantee a future. It only has to make the future feel conditional.
