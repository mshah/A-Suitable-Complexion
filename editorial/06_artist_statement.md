# Artist Statement

I began with photographs of my own family: weddings, celebrations, holidays and ordinary moments of being together. I wanted to look at the strange contrast between the richness of these relationships and the narrow categories used to describe complexions in Indian matrimonial culture.

I invented a rule. Everyone in the photograph receives one of seven colours, selected from a point at the middle of the nose. In some figures the skin is painted as a single flat field; in others it is left blank, with a number marking the colour that would be applied. Clothing, jewellery, faces and relationships remain recognisable. Every painting repeats the same chart.

A paint-by-numbers system seems harmless until the labels begin to resemble a system of social judgment. The categories are not scientific; the consistency is part of the fiction. I am interested in how easily an imposed measurement can be mistaken for an objective description.

The larger digital exhibition follows that judgment beyond the family album and into advertisements, corporate brands, beauty markets and health warnings. Companies have sold the possibility of becoming more desirable or successful through a change in complexion. I want to ask who benefits from that promise, and what we lose when belonging begins to resemble something that can be purchased.

The photographs are of real people I care about. This work is not a judgment of them. It is an attempt to make the system visible.

**Short version for cards:** I place a seven-shade matrimonial-style colour chart over photographs of my own family. Some skin is painted flat; some is left blank and numbered. The people stay recognisable. The system becomes impossible to ignore.
