01 / THE WORK

The family archive

The people are real. The numerical logic imposed on them is not.

Sixteen paintings selected for the main exhibition

Every source photograph was taken at a wedding. Twelve images are artist-selected finals; four remain working studies. This is a private review collection: final artwork approval and publication permissions are separate steps.

THE WEDDING AS A SYSTEM

The clothing celebrates individuality.
The chart reduces a person to a number.

A wedding photograph can hold intricate textiles, shared rituals and generations of belonging. The complexion chart interrupts that richness with a classification that only looks objective. The series preserves the expressive detail of dress while rendering skin in a single numbered shade.

A CLOSER LOOK · THE PERSON DOES NOT CHANGE

The Same Face, A Different Number

The complexion key suggests an orderly system: seven tones, seven labels, a supposedly consistent way of reading the body. But across these works, the artist is assigned different values—03 in one image, 05 in another, and in one case no colour at all. The contradiction is intentional. It reveals how arbitrary these classifications are, even when they are presented as objective, natural, or fair. The person does not change; the category does.

Compare Bridal Party, Gathering, Three Women, The Red Throne and The Unfilled Portrait. The same seven-shade key accompanies every image, but the artist does not retain one fixed classification.

All 16 artworks belong to this proposed main collection. “Final” indicates an artist-selected image, not confirmed public-display permission. Two earlier candidates are excluded from this site build, but their original artwork files remain available separately for the artist’s records.

AN INTERACTIVE CLASSIFICATION
INTERACTIVE · CLICK A SHADE / TOGGLE PAINT

Seven choices.
No neutral answer.

This is the fixed seven-colour key used throughout the series. The wording reflects a documented matrimonial classification; the hexadecimal colours and sample-point procedure are inventions of the artist. No shade represents a biological group or a measure of worth.

Select a swatch to see how a single colour turns into a whole-person label. Toggle between filled and unfilled to see the two permitted states in the paintings.

An echo from A-Thrice · Faceprint
“They turn your skin to paperwork and file you by your head.”

In the song this concerns biometric systems. In the paintings the filing system is familial, historical and socially constructed.

THE METHOD

Precision can make an invention look like a fact.

One colour per person. All visible skin is filled consistently, or all is left blank and numbered. Hair, clothing, jewellery, expressions and surroundings stay recognisable. Each painting repeats the exact same complexion key.

Some figures have no fill at all. Their assigned number makes the viewer aware of the temptation to complete the exercise.
