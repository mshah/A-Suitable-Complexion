The Chart and the Family

The family photograph is an act of recognition. Even before we know the people, we know the ritual: someone asks everyone to stand closer; a child moves; a hand settles on a shoulder; the camera records a temporary arrangement that becomes a lasting memory.

A Suitable Complexion begins there, with photographs of the artist's own family. The image is carefully retained, then subjected to a rule. Each person's complexion is matched to one of seven fixed colours at the middle of the nose. The chosen colour covers all visible skin—or the skin remains blank, with its number exposed. Hair, faces, eyes, jewellery, clothes, gestures and surroundings remain identifiable. The process resembles a paint-by-numbers book, but the numbering recalls the language of Indian matrimonial classification. S01

The seven labels are: Very Fair, Fair, Wheatish, Wheatish Medium, Wheatish Brown, Wheatish Dark and Dark. They were recorded as search categories on one matrimonial website in 2017. The swatch colours themselves are modern artistic choices, not a scientific scale or a reproduction of the site's original colours. S01 S23

The title borrows a question from Vikram Seth's A Suitable Boy: what makes a prospective partner suitable, and who has the authority to decide? This series changes the noun. The person remains complex; the category becomes absurdly simple.

The accompanying essays and archive look beyond the photographs. Colonial advertising circulated beauty creams across India and China. Post-independence marketing associated fairness with desirability and professional futures. Modern corporations now speak more often of radiance, brightness and glow. Across that history, another question appears: if the social meaning of complexion generates demand, who earns revenue from that meaning? S02 S03 S09

This is not a project about deciding what shade anyone truly is. It is about the authority that a chart borrows from its form, and the harm that can be done when the intimacy of a person's life is reduced to a purchasable idea of improvement.

Pull quote: The people belong to one another. The numbers belong to the system.
