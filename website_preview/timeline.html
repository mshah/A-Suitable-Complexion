From Snow to Glow

A selective history of complexion commerce, centered on India. This timeline distinguishes older social preferences from the industrial products that later monetized them. Items from Japan, Thailand, the Philippines and the UK supply comparison, not a claim that all Asian histories are identical. Year ranges and uncertain dates are marked explicitly.

Precolonial / early modern

Before industrial cosmetics · Beauty traditions precede commercial creams

India / Asia. Preferences for lighter skin appear in multiple historical contexts; the exhibition rejects a single-origin account. S02, S03

Editorial note: Do not assign a universal start date to colourism.

Late colonial period

1890s · Hazeline line sold as skin care

UK / global. British manufacturer Burroughs Wellcome marketed Hazeline products first around hygiene and skin care, ahead of later beauty and whitening language. S03, S05

Editorial note: Heritage/medical claims not identical to subsequent Indian ads.

c. 1909 · Snow enters the copy

UK / global. A British Hazeline Snow leaflet advertised the effect of making skin soft and white. S05

Editorial note: British item, not confirmed as India-specific.

Colonial India

1912 · Burroughs Wellcome opens Bombay branch

India. Global pharmaceutical advertising and local Indian marketing become connected through a formal branch. S03

Editorial note: Branch year documented in archival academic article.

1919 · Afghan Snow launches

India. E. S. Patanwala’s brand made a locally produced Indian beauty cream part of the category; snow imagery helped circulate complexion ideals. S04, S31

Editorial note: 1919 date is reported history; keep source attribution.

1930 · Hazeline Snow in Indian press

India. The Hindu carried Hazeline Snow product advertising in October 1930. S03

Editorial note: Indian original reproduced in Cambridge article; reproduction clearance pending.

Parallel Asian history

1937 · Shiseido Whitening Cream

Japan. Shiseido lists a Whitening Cream within its modern facial-care programme. S06

Editorial note: Comparative case, not evidence of identical cultural dynamics.

Colonial India

1940s · The modern woman appears in Hazeline advertising

India. Indian print advertisements increasingly paired creams with women and modern beauty imagery. S03

Editorial note: Historical interpretation drawn from academic archive research.

Early postcolonial

1947–1950 · Independence; whitening rhetoric survives

India. After independence, corporate discussions still promoted local appeal of Hazeline’s skin-whitening effect. S03

Editorial note: 1950 company correspondence documented in archival research.

Postcolonial mass market

1975–1978 · Fair & Lovely reaches the market

India. Reporting dates pilot/test marketing to March 1975; HUL’s own anniversary chronology dates its launch to 1978. S07, S32

Editorial note: Show both dates with source labels, not one artificially certain date.

1990s–2000s · From personal care to social aspiration

India. Beauty marketing links complexion with marriage, romance, self-confidence and work; competition expands. S08, S32

Editorial note: Advertising messages are not proof of real economic returns.

Regional campaigns

2008 · GlutaMAX “From ebony to Ivory”

Philippines. A documented ad uses a stark before/after complexion transformation. S25

Editorial note: Archival reproduction needs clearance.

Regulation and backlash

2014 · Fairness-advertising guidelines

India. ASCI warns against associating dark skin with unhappiness, inferior status, marriage disadvantage or reduced employment prospects. S08

Editorial note: A self-regulatory guideline, not proof violations ended.

Regional campaigns

2016 · “White makes you win”

Thailand. Seoul Secret’s Snowz supplement campaign causes criticism and is removed. S21

Editorial note: Avoid presenting one campaign as representative of all Thailand.

Rebranding

2019–2020 · Fair & Lovely becomes Glow & Lovely

India. HUL describes a 2019 messaging shift to glow and radiance, followed by 2020 renaming. S09, S28

Editorial note: Company states formulation evolved and product is not bleaching cream.

Public health

2023 · Mercury rule strengthens

Global. Minamata Convention parties decide to remove a 1ppm threshold from the cosmetics phase-out provision. S18

Editorial note: Implementation varies by country.

Consumer safety

2024–2026 · Illegal-product warnings continue

UK. Trading Standards warns about hazardous products; later alerts show illegal skin-lightening cosmetics in diverse retail settings and online. S20, S29

Editorial note: Geographic UK case study, not Asia prevalence.

Current market

2026 estimate · Two different market measures

Global / India. Global skin-lightening forecast: US$13.2bn; entire India skincare estimate: US$2.98bn. These are not comparable categories. S13, S14

Editorial note: Both figures from commercial firms; 2026 global forecast not observed revenue.
